Robots rarely learn from radar because real radar is expensive to record, hard to label, and tied to one sensor. Prompt-to-Radar predicts what a specific radar would measure in any scene and from any robot pose, so a robot can train its radar perception in a world model and take it back to the real world.

Abstract

Radar works in darkness, rain and glare, yet robots rarely learn from it. We present a generative radar world model that predicts what a specific radar would measure in any scene and from any robot pose. Scenes come from a video diffusion model trained on four driving datasets (nuScenes, Waymo Open, Argoverse 2 and BDD100K), a real video, or a robot's own map. They are ray-traced, passed through a circuit-level receive chain with an adaptive analog front end, and perceived by a state-space detector, SSMRadNet, that outputs obstacles, closing speeds and tracks. Cached ray tracing makes frames re-run 33–141× faster, so the radar hardware itself becomes a knob. As a late-breaking result we close the Real2Sim2Real loop on a LoCoBot: corridors the robot maps are rebuilt in the world model, 77 GHz radar rendered there trains perception for navigation, and the stack is deployed back on the physical robot.

Method

Five stages, from a sentence to a planner input

Each stage is a module with its own knobs. Ray tracing runs once per scene and is cached; everything after it runs live on every frame.

1 · GENERATE

Scene

A latent video diffusion transformer turns a prompt into driving scenes, or a drive video or robot map is used directly. Objects are lifted to a 3D layout.

2 · SIMULATE

Ray-traced radar

Sionna RT traces every echo from real geometry and radio materials; an FMCW or OFDM waveform, RF front end and 12-bit ADC run on top.

3 · ADAPT

Analog front end

The channel is low-rank, so an analog combiner sends 16 antennas to 12 ADCs along a rank-4 subspace tracked by AdaOja.

4 · PERCEIVE

SSMRadNet

A sample-wise state-space detector runs on the ADC data and returns each object's class, range, azimuth and radial velocity.

5 · NAVIGATE

Planner input

Detections link into tracks: free space ahead, closing speed for braking and gap decisions, and obstacles that persist.

Generated and real scenes

The boxes come from radar, not the camera

Both inputs below run through the same chain. The camera view is only for display: SSMRadNet sees simulated radar and its detections are projected back onto the video.

Late-breaking result

Real2Sim2Real on a LoCoBot

The robot carries a radar, 2D LiDAR, RGB-D and stereo cameras and an edge processor. We bring its own corridors into the world model, train there with exact labels, and deploy back on the robot.

Real

Collect and map

Drive the corridors, record all sensors, and build an occupancy map with pose.

Sim

Rebuild and train

Rebuild the map as a 3D scene, render 77 GHz radar along recorded and new trajectories with moved obstacles, and train SSMRadNet with exact labels.

Real

Deploy and navigate

Run the trained stack on the robot's edge processor and navigate seen and unseen corridors.

Hardware as a knob

Change the radar before you build it

Because only ray tracing is cached, the receive chain can be re-run on the same scene with different hardware. Lowering receiver bias here shows exactly what each milliwatt costs in the radar image.
